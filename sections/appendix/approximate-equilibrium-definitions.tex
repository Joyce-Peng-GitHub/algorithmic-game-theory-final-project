\section{Approximate-Equilibrium Definitions}
\label{app:equilibrium-definitions}

In an $m \times n$ bimatrix game $(\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{C})$, we define
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$\varepsilon$-NE ($\varepsilon$-Nash Equilibrium)}: A mixed strategy profile $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y})$ in a bimatrix game $(\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{C})$ is called an $\varepsilon$-NE if and only if:
        \begin{align*}
          \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) & \ge \max_{i \in [m]} \mathcal{R}(e_i, \mathbf{y}) - \varepsilon \\
          \mathcal{C}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) & \ge \max_{j \in [n]} \mathcal{C}(\mathbf{x}, e_j) - \varepsilon
        \end{align*}
        That is, the maximum gain of any player from a unilateral deviation is at most $\varepsilon$.

  \item \textbf{$\varepsilon$-WSNE ($\varepsilon$-Well-Supported Nash Equilibrium)}: A mixed strategy profile $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y})$ is called an $\varepsilon$-WSNE if and only if:
        \begin{align*}
          \mathcal{R}(e_i, \mathbf{y}) & \ge \max_{k \in [m]} \mathcal{R}(e_k, \mathbf{y}) - \varepsilon &  & \forall i \in \operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{x}) \\
          \mathcal{C}(\mathbf{x}, e_j) & \ge \max_{k \in [n]} \mathcal{C}(\mathbf{x}, e_k) - \varepsilon &  & \forall j \in \operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{y})
        \end{align*}
        That is, every pure strategy in the support of the current strategy is an $\varepsilon$-best response, i.e., the gain from deviating from it is at most $\varepsilon$.
\end{itemize}

It is easy to see that every $\varepsilon$-WSNE is an $\varepsilon$-NE (multiplying the inequalities for all pure strategies in the support by their probabilities and summing them up yields the two inequalities of an $\varepsilon$-NE) while the reverse is not necessarily true. Intuitively, in an $\varepsilon$-NE, a player may assign probabilities to some ``bad'' pure strategies and compensate for them with other ``good'' pure strategies. An $\varepsilon$-WSNE, on the other hand, requires that every pure strategy in the support is ``good''.
